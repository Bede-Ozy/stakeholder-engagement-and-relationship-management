ADMINISTRATIVE DIALOGUE

LISTEN → CLARIFY → REFLECT → RESPOND

STEP 1 LISTEN

Hear without interrupting or formulating immediate institutional defenses.

STEP 2 CLARIFY

Ask diagnostic questions to unpack technical, budgetary, or operational ambiguities.

STEP 3 REFLECT

Restate their core concerns to confirm accurate understanding before formulating policy.

STEP 4 RESPOND

Present a clear, evidence-based administrative position anchored in procedure.

INSTEAD OF:
“That’s not possible.”
Breeds cynicism & resistance
VS
USE:
“What specifically concerns you about the proposal?”
Reveals actionable obstacles & invites dialogue
Listen for: What they say • What they mean • What they need
CONFLICT DE-ESCALATION

DON'T LABEL. DIAGNOSE.

ANGRY
→ De-escalate

Lower your vocal pitch, validate frustration without admitting liability, separate emotion from administrative facts.

RESISTANT
→ Understand the concern

Probe for past institutional letdowns, loss of departmental turf, or genuine resource deficit.

SILENT
→ Create space

Invite structured offline input, ask direct open-ended questions, protect junior officers from pressure.

DOMINANT
→ Structure conversation

Enforce meeting standing orders, implement strict time allocations, redirect focus back to the collective agenda.

UNREASONABLE
→ Set boundaries

Anchor firmly on Public Service Rules, statutory mandates, legal remits, and documented service standards.

Don’t match emotional intensity with emotional intensity.

DECISION-MAKING FILTER

BEFORE YOU DECIDE, ASK:

WHO?

Who will be affected?

Direct, indirect, and future beneficiaries or vulnerable groups.

WHY?

What are their interests?

Motivations, priorities, institutional survival, and citizen stakes.

WHAT?

What evidence do we have?

Data, verifiable metrics, administrative precedents, and field feedback.

RISK?

What could go wrong?

Operational bottlenecks, political fallout, resistance, unintended consequences.

RULES?

What does procedure require?

Public Service Rules, procurement guidelines, statutory authorities.

IMPLEMENTATION?

Can this actually work?

Budgetary backing, departmental capacity, field-level practicality.

STATUTORY & RESEARCH REFERENCES

1. Nigerian Public Sector Framework

Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation (OHCSF)

Federal Public Service Rules (Revised Edition). Prescribing professional standards, efficiency, probity, meritocracy, and statutory administrative due process across Federal Ministries, Departments, and Agencies (MDAs).

SERVICOM Presidency (Service Compact with All Nigerians)

Service Charter Guidelines and Citizen Grievance Redress Mechanisms. Benchmarking public sector performance against citizen rights to timely, fair, honest, and effective administrative delivery.

Federal Republic of Nigeria

Freedom of Information (FOI) Act 2011. Mandating institutional transparency, proactive disclosure of public records, and structured citizen engagement in governance.

2. International Public Governance & Multilateral Standards

OECD (Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development)

Recommendation on Open Government (2017) & Citizen Participation Guidelines (2021). Outlining empirical evidence on how structured stakeholder engagement bolsters public trust, policy legitimacy, and cost-effective implementation.

World Bank Group

Environmental and Social Framework (ESS10: Stakeholder Engagement and Information Disclosure). Standardizing power-interest diagnostics, vulnerable group consultation, and continuous stakeholder feedback loops in public projects.

3. Behavioral Science, Emotional Intelligence & Decision Theory

Goleman, D. (1998, 2004)

Working with Emotional Intelligence & What Makes a Leader? Harvard Business Review. Establishing the 5-domain framework: Self-awareness, Self-regulation, Motivation, Empathy, and Social Skill as the chief determinant of leadership efficacy.

Mayer, J. D., Salovey, P., & Caruso, D. R. (2004)

“Emotional Intelligence: Theory, Findings, and Implications.” Psychological Inquiry. The four-branch ability model: Perceiving emotions, Facilitating thought, Understanding emotional dynamics, and Managing emotions.

Fisher, R., Ury, W., & Patton, B. (Harvard Negotiation Project)

Getting to Yes: Negotiating Agreement Without Giving In. Formulating the core distinction between rigid positional bargaining and interest-based consensus building in institutional negotiations.

Moore, Mark H. (Harvard Kennedy School)

Creating Public Value: Strategic Management in Government. The Strategic Triangle linking Authorizing Environment (stakeholders/rules), Operational Capacity, and Public Value Creation.
